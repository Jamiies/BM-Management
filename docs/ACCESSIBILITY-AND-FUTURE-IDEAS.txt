PART 2 — ADA / WCAG 2.1 AA COMPLIANCE PLAN
===============================================================================

NOTE ON THE LEGAL SIDE: "ADA compliant" for a website in practice means
conforming to WCAG 2.1 Level AA. That is the standard courts and the DOJ have
pointed to. Aiming at AA is the right target. This is a checklist for a
technical build, not legal advice — if the site becomes important to his
business, having an accessibility professional audit it is worth the money.

  [x] Hero photo contrast — RE-MEASURED 2026-08-10 from the actual rendered
      page. The earlier figures came from an analytical model of the image
      plus the overlay, and the model was WRONG for the gold eyebrow: it
      reported 6.49:1 where the real render was 4.46:1, i.e. an actual AA
      failure was being reported as a pass.

      HOW TO MEASURE THIS CORRECTLY (the method now used):
        1. Render the page normally.
        2. Render it again with `.hero-inner *{visibility:hidden}` — same
           geometry, no text.
        3. The glyph mask is the set of pixels that DIFFER between the two.
        4. Read background luminance from the TEXT-HIDDEN render.
      Step 4 is the important one. Sampling background from the normal render
      counts the anti-aliased edges of the glyphs as background, which makes
      white text look like it is sitting on a white background — a first
      attempt at this produced "1.18:1" for the headline, which was nonsense.
      The script is straightforward to rewrite; it lives only in scratch, so
      re-create it from this description if needed.

PART 4 — DESIGN DECISIONS MADE (so we don't re-litigate them)
===============================================================================

  * Navy + bronze/gold palette — reads as established and financial without
    being the generic blue every accounting site uses.
  * Serif headings + sans-serif body — traditional and credible for the
    profession, still easy to read.
  * System fonts, no external font loading — fast, no network dependency,
    no third-party privacy considerations. Revisit later if we want more
    typographic personality.
  * HERO PHOTOGRAPH — read this before touching the hero.

    Image:    "Silicon Mountain" by Sheila Sund
    Source:   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Silicon_Mountain.jpg
    Original: Flickr, taken 2013-05-31
    Licence:  CC BY 2.0  — https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/
    Original: 3401 x 2091. Wikimedia lists no additional restrictions.

    ATTRIBUTION IS A LICENCE CONDITION, NOT A COURTESY. CC BY 2.0 permits
    commercial use and modification, but only if credit is given. That is
    what the .photo-credit line in the footer of all five pages is doing.
    Do not delete it while this photo is in use.

    The file shipped is NOT the raw original. Two edits were made, both to
    solve a real accessibility problem rather than for looks:

      1. A graduated ND on the sky (30%, feathering out by 60% height) —
         moody dusk look, and it drops the sky's luminance.

      2. A HIGHLIGHT ROLLOFF above value 190, compressing toward 200.
         This is the important one. The sunlit tower faces in the original
         were blown to pure white (measured luminance 1.000 — literally
         255,255,255). No practical overlay fixes pure-white pixels: at a
         60% overlay the headline was landing at 3.2:1 against them, well
         under the 4.5:1 AA threshold. Darkening the whole image enough to
         compensate needed ~90% opacity, which veiled the mountains and
         defeated the point of using a photo.
         Rolling the highlights off instead fixed the contrast at the
         source and let the overlay come back DOWN to 55-74%, so the
         skyline and the snowcaps both stay visible.

    Note the overlay gets heavier toward the BOTTOM, which is backwards from
    the usual instinct. The sky is already graded down; the brightest thing
    left in frame is the lit buildings in the lower half.

    Two sizes ship: the 2000px version (151KB) and a 1100px version (53KB)
    swapped in below 800px via media query.

  * The card's skyline (print/skyline-card.svg) is deliberately a SEPARATE
    file from the website's hero SVG, not a copy with tweaks. They are drawn
    for opposite contexts — the hero sits behind a dark overlay on a light
    page, the card strip sits on solid navy. Keeping them separate means a
    change to one cannot silently break the other. If Denver's skyline is
    ever redrawn, both files need updating.

  * The original hand-built SVG hero is still in assets/images/ as
    hero-denver-rockies.svg (6.6KB). Kept as a fallback and as a reference
    for the layered-illustration look, in case Bill prefers an illustration
    to a photo. Not currently referenced by any page.
  * FAQ uses native <details>/<summary> rather than a custom JS accordion —
    accessible by default, works without JavaScript, less to maintain.
  * Only 5 pages, matching the 5 tabs requested. Contact lives in the footer
    and in CTAs on every page rather than as a sixth tab.

  * NAMING SPLIT (decided 2026-07-24). The business is BM Management; the
    person is Bill Mellenthin, CPA. The site leads with the PERSON and
    supports with the COMPANY, because clients hire a named CPA with twenty
    years of history, not a two-month-old LLC. So:
      - the header wordmark says BM Management, with his name beneath it
      - the home page H1 stays "Bill Mellenthin, CPA"
      - the hero eyebrow carries the company name
      - legal contexts (copyright, contracting) say "BM Management, LLC"
    The existing "BM" monogram already worked for the new name, which is
    convenient but was luck, not planning.
